Measles Hagelloch 1861, six-panel pre-fit panel.
A: first symptoms come a few days before the rash. The rash is what the physician recorded reliably for all 188 children, so that is what the model is compared against.
B: the running total reaches 188 and stops - every child in the village caught it.
C: three days in five had no new rash at all, then bursts. Clustered, not steady.
D: the raw counts carry a pattern; after the model almost nothing is left. Clean.
E: the village children split into three school groups; the mid-epidemic burst is the disease sweeping through classrooms.
F: the model captures the timing but draws a smoother, lower hump than reality, because it assumes everyone mixes evenly and a village with schools does not.
